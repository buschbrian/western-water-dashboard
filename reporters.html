Western Water Dashboard

For reporters

This page is for reporters and editors who use figures from this site. It tells you how to cite a figure, what the figures can support, and who to contact. The methods page gives the full method for every number.

How to cite a figure

Credit line: Source: [provider] data, compiled by the Western Water Dashboard (buschbrian.github.io/western-water-dashboard).

Name the provider first, because the measurements are theirs. Each reservoir page names its provider under "Measured by". This site collects the readings and does the arithmetic.

  • Give the date of the reading. Each reservoir page shows it as the reading date. The numbers change every morning, and some providers publish only once a month.
  • Link to the page that shows the figure. Each reservoir has its own page, for example Lake Powell. Its address stays the same from day to day.
  • Copy the address of a map view. The storage map, the snow page and the drought page keep the place you chose in the address.

What the main figures mean

Percent full
Storage now, divided by the reservoir's full level. The reservoir page tells you which full level it uses.
Normal for this week
The middle value for the same week in earlier years. The site compares against 1991 through 2020 unless the page names a different period.
History rank
How today's reading compares with the same date in each year since 2015. "1st-lowest of 12" means the lowest of those 12 years. Write "lowest for this time of year since 2015". Do not write "record low", because the record here starts in 2015.
Change in 30 days, change in 1 year
The difference between the newest reading and the reading nearest that many days before it.
Drought
The share of land in a drainage area at each class of the U.S. Drought Monitor's weekly map. A new map is published each Thursday.
Snow
Snow water, compared with the 1991 through 2020 median for the same day. Early in the season the normal is very small. Then the page gives the depth in inches instead of a percentage.

What the figures cannot support

  • Nothing here is a forecast. Every number is a measurement, a published assessment, or arithmetic on those.
  • People operate these reservoirs. A reservoir can fall because it releases water for deliveries or power. Ask the operator before you write that a watershed is drying.
  • The period you compare against changes the answer. Name the period when you give a percent of normal.
  • Readings are provisional. A provider can revise a reading later, and the next morning's data will carry the revision.
  • A combined total mixes reading dates. Each page that shows a total gives the range of dates behind it.
  • This is not an official product. Where this site and an agency disagree, the agency is right.

Data files

The public data API gives the same numbers as JSON files, with a description of each field. Each reservoir page also has a CSV download of that reservoir's readings.
