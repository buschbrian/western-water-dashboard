Drought on the ground

The rest of this site measures water. The drought page counts the land in each drought class each week. The storage map counts the water behind each dam. Neither one shows what the dry ground looks like. This page does, in photographs made in the Great Basin.

What a photograph proves

A photograph is evidence about one place on one day. It is not a measurement and it is not a trend. It cannot tell you how much of a drainage area is dry. Only the weekly measurements can do that.

So read the two together. The chart gives the extent, and the photograph gives the scale. A lake bed you can walk across is the same fact as a class on a map. It is the version a person can hold on to.

Places, and why there are no coordinates

No photograph on this page carries exact coordinates. Each one names its place at the coarsest level that still means something: a named lake bed, a valley, or a county.

Playa crusts, desert springs and shoreline flats are fragile ground. A tire track across a dry lake bed can stay there for years. A published point brings vehicles to places that cannot carry them.

Researchers who need a location for work can write to the address on the terms and license page.

Every photograph is a real capture

Each photograph carries a provenance block below it. The block names the place, the month and the photographer. It also states that the frame is a real capture with no generative content.

The processing is ordinary: exposure, color, and crop. Nothing is added to a frame and nothing is taken out of one. That claim is about provenance, not about tools.
